Sink Drains Slowly but Is Not Clogged in Magna, UT

A slow sink without a plain clog usually has a partial blockage, a vent problem or a slope issue. Here is how to tell.

Talk to a plumber.
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

By the Magna Express Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026.

A sink that drains slowly without a clear clog usually has a partial blockage further down the line, a vent problem that slows airflow, or a pipe with too little slope. Start by checking whether other fixtures are slow too. If only one sink is slow, the problem is local. If several are slow, the cause is in the shared branch or main line.

Is It One Fixture or Many?

Run the kitchen sink, the bathroom sink and the shower one at a time and watch each one. A single slow fixture points to its trap or the pipe just past it. Several slow fixtures on the same floor point to a shared branch. A slow drain at the lowest fixture in the house, such as a basement floor drain, points to the main line.

Vents: The Hidden Piece

Every drain needs air. A vent pipe, usually through the roof, lets air in behind the water so it flows freely. If the vent is blocked by a bird nest, leaves or frost, the drain gurgles and slows down, like a bottle held upside down. Winter frost can close a vent in a cold snap, then reopen when it warms.

Tests You Can Run

Simple checks before you call

  • Fill the sink and pull the stopper. Does it drain steadily or in surges?
  • Listen for gurgling at the toilet or tub when the sink drains.
  • Remove and clean the stopper and pop-up assembly.
  • Place a bucket and remove the P-trap to look for buildup.
  • Try a plunger with the overflow hole blocked by a wet rag.

Skip chemical drain openers. They can damage some pipes, they do not remove hair or grease well, and they leave caustic water in the pipe that is dangerous for a plumber to work around.

Magna Note: Moderately Hard Water and Film

Magna Water District's 2023 report classifies its hardness as moderately hard, with an average of 105 mg/L as calcium carbonate. The district's own definition of moderately hard is 61 to 120 mg/L.

Hardness reacts with soap to form a sticky film rather than a clean rinse. In a bathroom sink, that film coats the trap and pipe walls and catches hair, which is a common reason drains slow without ever clogging solid. Cleaning the pop-up and the trap on a regular schedule, and using a hair catcher, prevents most of it.

When a Cable or Jetter Is the Answer

A cable machine passes through the pipe and scrapes away buildup. A jetter scours the entire pipe wall with high-pressure water. For a slow drain that returns, a camera shows whether the pipe has a belly, a crack or root intrusion. See drain cleaning for cabling and hydro jetting for deeper cleaning.

When the Problem Is the Main Line

If the shower and the toilet are slow at the same time and the basement drain gurgles, the main sewer line may be partly blocked. That needs a larger cable or a jetter at the cleanout. Magna Water District provides sewer service, and the district's responsibility ends at the connection, so the lateral to your house is yours. Ask a plumber to camera it.

Customers in Kearns and West Valley City also see this pattern in older neighborhoods with long laterals.

Why Slope and Layout Matter

A drain pipe needs a gentle downhill slope. Too flat and solids settle. Too steep and water outruns the solids, leaving them behind. Older renovations sometimes include long flat runs or extra bends to reach a remodeled sink. If a drain has been slow since the day it was installed, the layout is the likely culprit, and cleaning only helps for a few weeks.

A plumber can show you the problem with a camera and suggest whether re-piping a short section is worth it.

A Careful Look at the P-Trap

Place a bucket

Set a bucket under the trap to catch water.

Loosen the nuts

Turn the slip nuts by hand or with channel pliers.

Remove and inspect

Look for hair, soap film and gunk inside the trap.

Clean and reassemble

Rinse the trap and refit it with the washers seated flat.

Test

Run water and check for leaks at each joint.

If the trap is clean and the drain is still slow, the problem is beyond it. Do not keep pushing a snake from the sink without knowing what you are hitting. A camera shows whether the pipe has a partial blockage, a low spot or a vent problem, so the fix targets the real cause.

Habits That Keep Drains Moving

Use a hair catcher in the shower and bathroom sink. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing. Run hot water for a few seconds after using the sink. Once a month, remove and rinse the pop-up stopper. These habits cost almost nothing, and they prevent most film and hair buildup that slows a drain.

Vent and Trap Rules in Plain Terms

Plumbing codes require each fixture to have a trap and a vent so sewer gas stays out and water drains freely. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. A drain that was installed without proper venting will keep slowing down no matter how often it is cleaned.
