UV Cured Pipe Lining for Magna, UT Homes

UV cured lining rebuilds a cracked drain from the inside. Here is how it works, when it fits and when it does not.

Talk to a plumber.
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

By the Magna Express Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026.

UV cured pipe lining repairs a damaged sewer or drain pipe by installing a new, joint-free pipe inside the old one. A glass fiber liner soaked in resin is pulled into the damaged section, inflated, and hardened by a train of ultraviolet lights. The result is a rigid inner pipe that seals cracks and blocks roots, and nobody has to trench across your lawn or driveway to get it.

How UV Cured Lining Works

The process starts with cleaning. A plumber clears roots and buildup with a cable machine or a jetter, then runs a camera to confirm the pipe is round, continuous and free of obstructions. A liner that goes into a dirty or collapsed pipe will not seat properly, so this step decides whether the whole job works.

Next, a liner made of fiberglass cloth and light-sensitive resin is pulled through from an access point, usually a cleanout. Air pressure inflates it against the pipe wall. A UV light train then travels the length of the liner and cures the resin in place.

Clean the line

Roots, grease and scale are removed so the liner can seat tightly.

Camera the pipe

The plumber records the run and marks where the damage starts and ends.

Pull and inflate the liner

A resin-soaked liner is pulled in and pressed against the pipe wall.

Cure with UV light

A light train hardens the resin from end to end.

Camera again

A final recording shows a smooth lined pipe, and side connections are reopened.

UV Cured Versus Other Lining Methods

Other lining systems use felt liners and hot water or steam to cure the resin. They work well on many jobs, but they take longer to cure and depend on tight temperature control. UV curing is faster, and the glass fiber liner can be thinner for the same strength, so the pipe loses less diameter.

What Lining Fixes and What It Cannot

Lining suits cracks, open joints, minor root intrusion and surface corrosion. It does not suit a collapsed pipe, a severely offset joint or a deep belly where water sits. A liner follows the shape of the old pipe, so a sag stays a sag. The camera tells you which situation you have, and an honest plumber will say when lining is the wrong tool.

Lining is a poor fit when

  • The pipe is crushed, flattened or broken into pieces.
  • A joint is offset enough that the camera cannot pass.
  • A low spot holds water along the run.
  • The line has many sharp bends that a liner cannot follow.
  • A repair would need the pipe to be larger, not the same size.

Magna Note: Locates and Lining

Lining mostly avoids trenching, but a job often still needs a small access pit at one or both ends. Utah requires a utility locate before digging, and Blue Stakes of Utah says to request one three business days ahead, with marks valid for 21 calendar days. Call 811 early so a pit does not delay the day you planned for the lining crew.

For homeowners in Magna, that rule is the main schedule driver for a lining job, since the crew cannot dig until the marks are down. If the plumber needs an access pit in the front yard, ask who requests the locate and when the marks will be placed.

What Lining Costs and What Drives It

The price depends on how much pipe is lined, the pipe diameter, the access points and the surface above. A short repair under a flower bed is not the same project as a long run under a driveway. Expect a written scope with the footage, the access plan and what restoration is included. Ask what happens if the camera finds a problem midway, since a surprise belly or offset can change the plan.

Insurance rarely covers lining for wear and root damage. A documented sudden event, such as ground movement, may be treated differently, so read your policy before you decide.

Questions to Ask Before You Agree to Lining

Ask the plumber

  • Can I see the camera footage of the damage and the proposed liner length?
  • What material and resin does the liner use?
  • How will side connections be reopened?
  • Will you camera the finished pipe and give me the recording?
  • What is the plan if the pipe turns out to be worse than the first camera run showed?

When Lining Is Not the Answer

Sometimes a homeowner calls expecting lining and learns they need a section replaced. That is not a failed diagnosis. It is the point of the camera. If your pipe has collapsed, the better path is covered under sewer line repair, which explains spot repair, bursting and full replacement. If the pipe is only clogged, sewer line cleaning may be all you need.

Homes in Kearns and West Valley City have a similar mix of older clay and iron laterals, which is where lining is most often discussed. The key is to measure the damage first and choose the repair second.

Reading the Camera Footage Like a Pro

Ask to see the footage before you decide, and ask the plumber to narrate it. Fine white threads are root hairs. A dark line along the pipe wall is usually a crack. A flat, shiny pool means a belly that holds water. A black gap at a joint means the sections have separated. Each tells you something about whether lining will work.

Counting footage also helps you compare quotes. If two companies describe the same damage differently, ask both to point to the frame on the recording.

Standards Behind Liner Materials

Pipe lining materials are tested against published standards, commonly those developed by ASTM International. Ask the contractor which standard the liner meets and request the product data sheet. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it.
