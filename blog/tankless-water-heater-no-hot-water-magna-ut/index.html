Tankless Water Heater No Hot Water in Magna, UT

When a tankless heater stops giving hot water, a short checklist usually finds the cause. Here is the order to check it in.

Talk to a plumber.
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

By the Magna Express Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026.

If your tankless water heater is not producing hot water, check four things first: the display for an error code, the power, the gas or breaker supply and the isolation valves. If any of those is off, you may fix it in minutes. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 before anyone else.

If the checklist below does not help, the cause is probably scale, a failed sensor or an ignition fault, and a plumber can fix it.

Check These First, in This Order

Read the display

Write down any error code. The manufacturer's manual explains it.

Check power

Confirm the outlet or breaker is on and the unit's display lights up.

Check gas or electricity

A closed gas valve or tripped breaker stops the unit from firing.

Check the isolation valves

After service, valves are sometimes left closed. Open them fully.

Run a single tap

Open one hot tap fully so the flow is high enough to start the unit.

Low Flow: The Most Common Culprit

A tankless unit only fires when water moves fast enough. A clogged faucet aerator, a partly closed valve or a fouled inlet screen can drop the flow below that threshold. Unscrew the aerator at the tap, rinse it and test again. Then check the unit's inlet screen, which catches sediment and can clog after construction work or a water main repair.

Scale: The Slow Cause

Dissolved minerals deposit inside the heat exchanger. The passages narrow, flow drops, and the unit overheats and shuts down or delivers lukewarm water. A descaling flush with a small pump and a descaling solution clears it. If the unit is several years old and has never been flushed, scale is the first suspect.

A flush also removes the loose grit that can jam a flow sensor.

Magna Note: Water Chemistry Varies

Magna Water District's 2023 report gives hardness as calcium carbonate at an average of 105 mg/L, but the range runs from 58 to 185 mg/L. The district describes the average as moderately hard, and the spread shows that the water you get in one month is not the same as the water you get in another.

For a tankless heater, a wide range means scale builds at an uneven rate. A unit might look fine one year and struggle the next. The practical answer is a descaling schedule based on what the unit tells you rather than the calendar alone, and a quick check of flow and temperature rise whenever performance changes.

Ignition, Gas and Venting Faults

A gas unit that tries to light and fails may have a dirty igniter, a gas supply problem or a blocked vent. Do not try to open the sealed combustion area yourself. Vent problems are serious, since exhaust must leave the house. If the unit shows a venting error, stop using it and call. Carbon monoxide is odorless, so a working detector near the heater is essential.

When to call right away

  • The smell of gas near the unit.
  • Soot, scorch marks or a burning smell.
  • A carbon monoxide alarm.
  • Water leaking from the unit or its connections.
  • Repeated error codes after you reset it once.

When a Repair Is Not Worth It

A well-maintained tankless heater can last many years, but a unit with a cracked heat exchanger or a failed board may cost nearly as much to fix as to replace. A plumber can tell you which side of that line you are on. For diagnosis and repair of any heater, see water heater repair. For a new unit, tankless water heater explains the options.

Homeowners in Kearns and West Valley City hit the same issues. If you rely on the heater for the whole house, treat a cold shower as a reason to act soon, particularly in winter.

The Cold Water Sandwich and Intermittent Heat

If your hot water comes in pulses, the unit may be shutting down and restarting. It can happen when flow hovers just above the minimum, when a mixing valve is set too low, or when a recirculation loop crosses hot and cold lines. Opening a second fixture sometimes makes it worse. Try running one tap at a steady flow and see whether the pattern changes.

What a Plumber Brings to a Service Call

A thorough tankless diagnosis includes

  • Reading the unit's error history, not only the current code.
  • Measuring gas pressure at the unit while it fires.
  • Checking inlet and outlet temperatures to see the actual rise.
  • Inspecting the vent, condensate drain and air intake.
  • Flushing the heat exchanger and cleaning the inlet screen.

A unit that has never had a flush often needs one before anything else is tested, since scale can make every other reading look wrong. Ask the plumber to record the temperature rise before and after cleaning so you can see the difference.

Keeping a Log

Write down the date, the error code and what you were doing when the heater stopped. A short log shows patterns, such as shutdowns during a certain cycle or after a cold night. A plumber who sees the log can find the cause faster.

Manufacturer Guidelines and Code

Tankless heaters come with manufacturer guidelines for flushing, venting and clearances, and following them protects your warranty. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it.
