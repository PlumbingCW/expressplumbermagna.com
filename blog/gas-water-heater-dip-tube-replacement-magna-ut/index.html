Gas Water Heater Dip Tube Replacement in Magna, UT

A broken dip tube makes a gas water heater run out of hot water fast. Here is how to confirm it and what the repair involves.

Talk to a plumber.
(385) 488-1892
☎ Call Now

By the Magna Express Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026.

A dip tube is a plastic pipe inside the top of your water heater that sends incoming cold water to the bottom of the tank. If it cracks or breaks, cold water mixes with hot water at the top, and your showers turn lukewarm or run out quickly. It is a cheap part, but the repair requires draining the tank, so many people call a plumber.

What the Dip Tube Does

Cold water enters at the top of the tank through the dip tube, which carries it down to the bottom near the burner. The water heats, rises and leaves through the hot outlet at the top. With the tube intact, hot water stays on top and cold water stays on the bottom. With the tube broken, cold water enters near the top and dilutes the hot water.

Signs of a Failed Dip Tube

Lukewarm water

Hot water that never gets as hot as it used to.

Short showers

Hot water runs out much faster than before.

Plastic bits

Small white or gray flecks in faucet aerators.

Normal tank temperature

The tank feels hot but the output is mild.

Plastic pieces in aerators are a strong clue. They are fragments of the tube that have been carried through the lines.

How the Replacement Works

Shut off gas and water

The burner is turned off and the cold supply closed.

Drain the tank

Water is drained through the hose bib at the bottom.

Disconnect the cold inlet

The inlet nipple and its fittings are removed.

Remove and replace the tube

The old tube is pulled out and a new one installed.

Refill and relight

The tank is refilled and the pilot or igniter restarted, then leaks are checked.

Many heaters have the tube attached to the cold inlet nipple. In some units it is a separate piece. A plumber knows which type you have and has the right replacement. Fittings that are corroded or seized sometimes need to be replaced with the tube.

Magna Note: Sulfate and Odors

Magna Water District's 2023 report lists sulfate at an average of 51.3 mg/L, with a maximum of 118.0 mg/L. Sulfate is not a health concern at that level, but it matters for one common complaint in water heaters.

Sulfate can feed bacteria that produce a rotten egg smell in some tanks, usually in the hot water only. That is an anode and bacteria issue, not a dip tube problem, and replacing the tube will not fix it. Tell the plumber what you notice, whether it is temperature, smell or flakes, so the right problem is solved.

Repair or Replace the Heater

If the heater is under about eight years old and otherwise healthy, a dip tube is a sensible repair. If the tank is older, leaking or heavily scaled, replacing the heater may be wiser. Look at the manufacture date on the label. Gas heaters often last about a dozen years, depending on water and care.

If you are comparing, see water heater repair and water heater replacement.

Homes in Taylorsville and West Jordan with older heaters often see this problem first. The earlier you catch it, the less likely a failure becomes an emergency.

Sediment, Anode Rods and the Rest of the Tank

While the tank is drained for a dip tube, it is a good time to check the anode rod and flush sediment. The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes instead of the tank. If it is mostly gone, replace it, since the tank itself comes next. Flushing removes scale and sediment that insulate the bottom of the tank and make the burner work harder.

Cost Logic: Fix, Flush or Replace

A dip tube is a low-cost part, but the labor is the same as any tank drain. If the heater is also old, scaled and leaking at the drain valve, spending money on a tube may buy only a few months. Ask the plumber for a straight answer on remaining life. Your age, tank condition and gas control all enter that call.

A Quick Home Test for Mixing

Run hot water

Open a hot tap at the sink nearest the heater for two minutes.

Feel the pipes

Touch the hot outlet and cold inlet at the top of the heater, carefully.

Check the temperature

Compare the water temperature at the tap to the thermostat setting.

Note the hot supply

Time how long hot water lasts in a shower from a cold start.

Write it down

Share these notes with the plumber.

A tank set to a high temperature that gives only warm water points toward a mixing problem, such as a failed dip tube. A tank that gives hot water but too little of it points toward sediment, a failing element or an undersized unit. Notes make the diagnosis faster and cheaper.

Planning Ahead for the Next Heater

When you replace a heater, ask which dip tube material the new model uses and whether the cold inlet can be reached for service. Choose a unit with a decent warranty on the tank and keep the paperwork. Flush the tank once a year, test the relief valve and write the install date on the heater with a marker, so you always know its age.

Safety Guidelines for the Repair

Draining and refilling a gas water heater affects the gas controls, so manufacturers publish guidelines on shutting off gas and relighting. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it.

When to call a plumber

  • You smell gas near the heater.
  • The pilot will not stay lit after the tank is refilled.
  • Water leaks from the relief valve or the tank.
  • The tank is more than ten years old.
  • You cannot shut off the cold supply or gas safely.
