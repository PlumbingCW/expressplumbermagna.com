Garbage Disposal Air Switch Repair for Magna, UT Homes

If the button on your counter does nothing, the problem is often the air switch, not the disposal. Here is how to test it.

Talk to a plumber.

By the Magna Express Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026.

A garbage disposal air switch is the small button on your countertop or sink. Pressing it sends a puff of air through a thin tube to a switch under the sink, which powers the disposal. If the button clicks but nothing happens, the problem is usually a cracked tube, a failed switch module or a tripped reset on the disposal. A few simple tests can tell you which.

How an Air Switch Works

An air switch has three parts: the button, an air tube and a switch module under the sink. The disposal plugs into the module, and the module plugs into an outlet. When you press the button, air pressure closes a contact inside the module and power flows to the disposal. There is no electricity at the button, which makes it safe to use with wet hands.

Test It in This Order

Check the reset

Press the red reset button on the bottom of the disposal.

Check the outlet

Make sure the module is plugged in and the outlet has power, including the GFCI.

Check the tube

Look for kinks, cracks or a loose connection at the button and the module.

Test the button

Pull the tube off the module, blow into it and listen for a click at the module.

Bypass the switch

Briefly plug the disposal directly into the outlet to see whether the disposal itself runs.

If the disposal runs when plugged in directly, the air switch is the problem. If it does not, the disposal is the problem. Unplug the unit before touching anything inside it, and never reach into the grinding chamber.

Replacing the Air Switch

An air switch kit includes the button, the tube and the module. The new button mounts in the same hole in the countertop or sink. The tube runs below to the module. Make sure the tube is not pinched in a cabinet door or around plumbing. A longer tube may reduce the pressure, so keep it as short as practical.

Some disposals are hardwired to a wall switch instead. If your kitchen has no air switch, the problem is a wall switch or the wiring, and an electrician may need to be involved.

When the Disposal Itself Has Failed

A unit that hums, leaks from the bottom or trips the breaker repeatedly is a candidate for replacement. Most disposals last around a decade. A leak from the body means the seal has failed, and no repair will hold. If you are replacing, bring the old unit's mounting type and horsepower to choose a compatible new one.

Wiring and Outlet Basics

A disposal needs a standard grounded outlet under the sink or a hardwired connection. The air switch module plugs into that outlet. If you find the module warm, buzzing or scorched, unplug it and replace it. Never use an extension cord for a disposal. If the outlet is not protected by a ground fault circuit interrupter near a sink, an electrician can add one.

A Cheap Way to Prevent the Next Failure

Disposal habits that protect the switch and the motor

  • Run cold water for a few seconds before and after grinding.
  • Press the button once and wait for the unit to spin up before adding food.
  • Feed scraps slowly instead of dumping them in.
  • Keep fibrous and starchy foods out of the unit.
  • Check the tube each year for kinks where cabinet doors rub.

Small habits matter because a jammed motor can overheat and trip the reset, which looks like a switch problem even though the switch is fine.

Choosing Between an Air Switch and a Wall Switch

Switch types compared
TypeAdvantageDrawback
Air switchNo electricity at the sink, easy to place on the counterTube and button can crack.
Wall switchSimple and durableNeeds wiring near the sink, can be reached with wet hands.
Wireless switchNo tube, flexible placementNeeds batteries and a receiver.

If your kitchen has a countertop that is difficult to drill, a wall switch or a wireless model avoids a hole in the stone. Check local electrical rules before changing the wiring, and use a licensed electrician when a new circuit is needed.

When to Stop and Call

Stop and call a plumber if the disposal leaks, smokes, smells hot or trips the breaker repeatedly. Do not keep resetting a unit that trips, since the motor or wiring may be failing. If you are unsure about the electrical side, an electrician or plumber can test the outlet and the module. Safety matters more than saving a service call.
