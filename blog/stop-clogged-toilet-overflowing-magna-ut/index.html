How to Stop a Clogged Toilet From Overflowing: A Magna, UT Homeowner Guide

If the bowl is rising, act in this order. These steps stop the flow and limit the damage before the plumber arrives.

Talk to a plumber.

By the Magna Express Plumbing team. Published November 13, 2026.

If a toilet is about to overflow, do not flush again. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water from entering the bowl, then turn the small valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise to shut off the supply. Those two steps stop new water. Then plunge. If other drains in the house are backing up, call right away, because the problem is likely the main line.

The First Ninety Seconds

Stop flushing

Do not flush again, even if the bowl seems to be draining.

Close the flapper

Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down into its seat.

Shut the supply

Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

Put down towels

Protect the floor and move rugs away.

Gather a plunger

Use a flange plunger, which seals better in a toilet drain.

How to Plunge Correctly

Press the plunger into the bowl so the rubber flange sits in the drain opening. Push down gently first to release air, then pump with firm, steady strokes while keeping the seal. After ten or so strokes, pull the plunger back sharply. If water drains, the clog is gone. If not, repeat a few times before moving to a closet auger.

Is It the Toilet or the Main Line?

Signs the main line is blocked

  • Water backs up in the shower or tub when you flush.
  • A floor drain in the basement gurgles or fills.
  • Several fixtures drain slowly at once.
  • Plunging moves water but never clears.
  • A sewer smell with no obvious source.

A main line blockage will not respond to a plunger, and flushing again can push sewage into the house. Stop using water and call. A plumber can clear the line from the cleanout, as explained on the sewer line cleaning page.

Magna Note: Who to Call and When

Magna Water District provides drinking water and sewer service, and its website lists office hours of 7:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m., Monday through Thursday, except for holidays. That means the district's office is not staffed on Fridays or evenings.

A backed-up toilet rarely waits for office hours. If the problem is inside your house or on your lateral, you need a plumber rather than the district anyway, and our line is open 24/7. If you suspect a blockage in the street main, such as sewage backing up in several nearby homes at once, ask the district what its after-hours process is, and tell the plumber what you learn.

Cleaning Up Safely

Sewage can carry bacteria. Wear gloves and boots, open windows for ventilation and keep children and pets away. Remove standing water with towels or a wet vacuum, then disinfect hard surfaces with a household disinfectant. Porous materials such as carpet and drywall that stay wet may need removal. Photograph everything for your insurer first.

When a Toilet Needs Repair or Replacement

A toilet that clogs often may have a partial blockage in the drain, or the fixture may be old. A plumber can check the flange, the wax ring and the drain. See toilet repair for repair and replacement. If the clog is a stubborn kitchen or bath drain, drain cleaning helps.

Tools Worth Keeping in the House

A small toilet rescue kit

  • A flange plunger, not a flat cup plunger.
  • A closet auger sized for toilets.
  • Rubber gloves and boots.
  • Old towels and a bucket.
  • A flashlight, in case the problem happens at night.

If the Water Is Already on the Floor

If the bowl has overflowed, stop the supply and move people away from the wet area. Turn off power to nearby outlets if water has reached them and you can reach the panel safely. Clean-water overflow from the tank is less of a hazard than sewage from a blocked drain. Treat sewage as contaminated and clean it with gloves and disinfectant.

Call your insurer if the water has soaked drywall or flooring. Sewer backup coverage is often an endorsement, so check your policy.

Preventing Repeat Clogs

Most toilet clogs are preventable. Use no more paper than needed, flush wipes never, and teach children that toys and cotton products belong in the trash. If your toilet clogs often with normal use, a plumber can check the flange, the trap and the drain for a partial blockage or a design problem.

Why Flushing Again Backfires

A toilet bowl holds a set volume of water. When the drain is blocked, flushing adds a full tank on top of a full bowl, and the extra water has nowhere to go but the floor. Waiting a few minutes lets the level drop on its own in a partial blockage, and it gives you time to shut the supply. A bowl that does not drop at all needs the plunger or a plumber.

When to Call at Night

If sewage is rising in more than one fixture, if water is reaching electrical outlets, or if you cannot stop the supply, call the 24/7 line without waiting for morning. Stopping the damage early often decides whether you are mopping a bathroom or replacing flooring. If the overflow is only a clog in a single toilet and you can contain it, you may be able to wait until a daytime visit.

Safe Cleanup Guidelines

Public health agencies publish guidelines for cleaning up sewage, such as wearing gloves, ventilating and disinfecting hard surfaces. Follow them. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it.
