A failing sewer line gives few warnings before it backs up into the house. Gurgling drains, a sewage smell in the yard or a floor drain that fills when you do laundry all point to a damaged lateral. When sewage reaches the house, it is an emergency, and our 24/7 line can send a plumber at any hour.

While you wait, stop using water, keep people away from the spill, and do not run the washer. Wear gloves and boots if you must enter the wet area.

Sewage in the House: What to Do First

Stop all water use, including toilets, showers and laundry. Open a cleanout if you know where it is, since pressure relief can slow the backup. Photograph the damage for insurance before any cleanup. Call the emergency line so a plumber can clear the blockage and then camera the pipe to see the cause.

Four Ways a Sewer Line Fails

Roots

Fine roots enter through joints and grow into a mat that catches paper and waste.

Cracks

Ground movement or heavy loads split the pipe, letting soil in and sewage out.

Bellies

A low spot holds water and solids, and drains slowly forever.

Collapse

Old clay or cast iron crushes, and the line stops draining.

Utah's active fault zone and the March 2020 magnitude 5.7 earthquake near Magna are reasons to camera an older line after strong shaking. The state earthquake site reports small cracks and sand boils near the epicenter, which shows the ground moved. A camera costs far less than discovering a break after a backup.

Trenchless lining inserts a resin-soaked liner that cures into a new pipe inside the old one. It avoids trenching across a lawn or driveway and, when the host pipe is intact, gives a joint-free surface roots cannot enter. Epoxy lining under a slab is a niche option, and the camera decides whether it suits your line.

Magna Soils, Old Lines and Newer Subdivisions

The oldest homes near Main Street may still use clay or cast iron laterals, which corrode and crack with age. Newer subdivisions use PVC, which resists roots better but can settle or be crushed during construction. Both show up in our camera work.

Magna Water District provides drinking water and sewer service, so it is the first call about the main in the street. Before you hire anyone, ask whether the lateral is your responsibility from the house to the main. That responsibility line, and any city permit, affects who pays and who digs. Call the building department for your city.

What a good sewer diagnosis includes

  • Camera footage with distance marked from the cleanout.
  • A locator mark on the surface so the dig point is exact.
  • A written description of the damage and every repair route.
  • A price for each route before you decide.
  • A note on whether a permit or utility locate is needed.

Permits, Locates and Code

Excavation near a sewer lateral usually needs a utility locate and may need a permit, and the repair itself is plumbing work. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. If your lateral crosses a public right of way, the city or the sewer district may add inspection steps, so ask who inspects before the trench is closed.
