Water Softener Installation in Magna, UT

Moderately hard water leaves scale on fixtures and inside heaters. We test, size and install a softener so you do not buy more salt than you need.

White crust on faucets, spotted glasses and a water heater that fails early are all signs of hard water. Magna Water District's 2023 consumer confidence report listed average hardness of about 105 mg/L, or roughly 6 grains per gallon, which is moderately hard. Whether you need a softener depends on how much it bothers you and your fixtures.

What Hardness Means in Magna

Water between 60 and 120 mg/L is called moderately hard. At that level, scale builds slowly. The district also reported total dissolved solids near 639 mg/L. Your tap may differ from the average depending on which source the district blends that month, so test your own water.

Scale insulates the bottom of a tank water heater, so it runs longer to heat the same water. See water heater repair and tankless water heater for how scale affects them.

Salt Softeners and Alternatives

A salt-based softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium and delivers truly soft water. It needs salt and a drain connection for regeneration. Salt-free conditioners change how scale forms but do not remove hardness. Some Utah communities limit discharge from softeners, so ask your sewer district before choosing a system.

If anyone in the house is on a sodium-restricted diet, ask a doctor about softened water.

Salt, Water Use and Ongoing Cost

A salt-based softener regenerates by flushing brine through the resin tank, which uses salt and a volume of water. Efficient metered models regenerate only when the resin is spent, which saves both. Check the model's salt efficiency rating and ask how often it will regenerate at your household size.

Where Softened Water Should and Should Not Go

Many homes keep an outdoor hose bib and a kitchen drinking tap on unsoftened water. Softened water is not harmful to lawns in small amounts but salty water can stress plants over time. A plumber can set up the bypass so only the water you want softened gets treated. The right layout is a short conversation during the site visit.

Standards and Sodium

Water treatment equipment sold for drinking water is often certified to NSF/ANSI standards, so look for the certification on the label. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. Softeners also need a legal drain connection with an air gap, and some sewer districts regulate brine discharge, so ask before you install.
