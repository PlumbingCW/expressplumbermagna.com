The Housing Behind the Calls

Kearns grew from Camp Kearns, an Army Air Corps training base that opened in 1942, and much of the housing followed in the postwar decades. The base itself is gone, but the neighborhoods that replaced it are mostly mid century and later. Homes from that period tend to have copper supply lines in the walls and clay or cast iron sewer laterals underground.

Copper lasts a long time but develops pinholes where the water is aggressive. Clay and iron laterals crack or let roots in. Both show up as repeat calls.

Kearns Improvement District and Your Service Line

Kearns Improvement District provides water and sewer service in most of Kearns, according to the district. The district owns the main and the meter, and you maintain the line from the meter to the house. If water is surfacing in your yard, close the shutoff at the house and call. A plumber will locate the break and explain whether it is on your side of the meter.

Because Kearns is metered by its own district, bills and notices come from the district. If a bill jumps with no change in use, leak detection is usually the next step.

Older Pipes Leave Clues

Clues in a mid century house include water that runs brown after sitting, a gurgle in a tub when the washing machine drains, and green stains around a copper joint. None of those is an emergency by itself, but each tells you where the next one may come from. Writing down when and where you notice them helps the plumber diagnose a pattern.

Seasonal Calls in Kearns

Winter brings frozen hose bibs and split lines in garages. Spring brings sump pit problems and sewer backups when the ground is saturated. Summer brings sprinkler line leaks and water heater strain. Fall is the best time to disconnect hoses, drain outdoor lines and test your main shutoff.
