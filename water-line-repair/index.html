A broken water service line can flood a yard or silently drain the meter. Signs include a soggy patch in the lawn when it has not rained, a sudden drop in pressure, a spinning meter with every fixture off, or a higher bill with no change in use. If water is surfacing, close the valve at the house and call the 24/7 line.

Who Owns the Line

Ownership of the service line differs by utility. In many places the utility owns the line to the meter and the homeowner owns the line from the meter to the house, but you should confirm that with your water provider. Magna Water District serves much of Magna, and parts of the area are served by other systems such as Kearns Improvement District or Granger-Hunter Improvement District, so ask which applies to your address.

Signs of a Failing Service Line

Wet ground

Soggy or sunken soil between the meter and the house.

Low pressure

Pressure falls at every fixture at once.

Spinning meter

The meter moves with all water off.

Discolored water

Rust or dirt after a pressure change.

High bills

Usage rises with no change in habits.

You can test for a leak by turning every fixture off and watching the meter. If the dial moves, water is going somewhere. A plumber can narrow it to the service line or the house plumbing.

Old galvanized steel service lines corrode from the inside and restrict flow long before they break. Modern copper, PEX or HDPE lines last far longer. A trenchless method pulls a new line through the old route, saving driveways and mature trees.

Digging in Utah: Locates and Permits

Utah requires a utility locate before you dig. Call 811 or use Blue Stakes of Utah at least three business days ahead, and the marks stay valid for 21 calendar days. Your city or district may also require a permit for a service line replacement, so check before work begins.

Soil near the Oquirrh foothills can be rocky, which slows trenching. Valley floor areas near the lake plain have different soil, and both affect how the route is planned.

Pressure matters too. A pressure reducing valve protects fixtures and joints. If your pressure runs high, the plumber may recommend one when the line is replaced. A burst inside the house is covered on the burst pipe repair page.

Pressure, Valves and What Happens at the House

Where the service line enters the house there should be a main shutoff valve and, in many homes, a pressure reducing valve. Both age. A main valve that will not close completely turns every plumbing repair into a bigger job. If yours is stuck, it is worth replacing during a line repair so you can isolate the house in an emergency.

Protecting the New Line

A new service line needs bedding material around it, correct depth for the soil and frost conditions, and a tracer wire so it can be found later. Compaction of the trench matters, since settling soil can stress the pipe. Ask the plumber how deep the line will sit and how the trench will be backfilled, and take photos before it is covered.

Service Line Rules

Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. The service line from the meter to the house is private plumbing, and replacing it usually needs a permit and an inspection of the trench. Ask the water district whether it requires its own inspection at the meter connection.
