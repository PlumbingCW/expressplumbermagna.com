A garbage disposal that hums and will not turn, or leaks onto the cabinet floor, is a small appliance with big consequences when it fails. Water under the sink can ruin cabinets overnight. If it is leaking, switch it off, shut the sink's supply valves and call the 24/7 line. We handle disposal emergencies at night and on weekends.

A Leaking Disposal: What to Do First

Turn the unit off at the switch, and if you can, unplug it under the sink. Place a bowl or towels under the leak, and shut the sink valves if the faucet is also dripping. Do not put your hand into the disposal. The reset button is on the bottom of the unit and a hex wrench socket sits underneath.

A leak from the body means the unit is finished. The seal between the grinding chamber and the motor has failed, and no patch lasts. Leaks at the flange or discharge are often repairable.

Air Switches and Dishwasher Connections

An air switch is the small button on the countertop that powers the disposal by a puff of air through a tube. When the unit does not start, a cracked tube or a failed switch is the cause. The dishwasher drain also connects to the disposal, and a clog there sends water into the dishwasher.

If the sink backs up into the dishwasher, the problem is usually the disposal outlet or the kitchen branch line. The plumber clears the branch and checks the air gap. See drain cleaning for kitchen drain clogs.

What Not to Grind in Magna Kitchens

Grease, coffee grounds, eggshell membranes, rice, pasta and fibrous vegetables like celery clog drain lines downstream of the disposal. In older homes with rougher drain pipe, those clogs form faster. The mineral film from moderately hard water, which Magna Water District reported at an average of 105 mg/L hardness in 2023, makes grease cling even more.

Disposal care that prevents calls

  • Run cold water before, during and after grinding.
  • Feed scraps in small amounts rather than a full load.
  • Never put grease, coffee grounds or fibrous scraps down.
  • Grind a few ice cubes now and then to scrape the chamber.
  • Replace the unit when the housing leaks.

Choosing a Replacement Disposal

Horsepower and feed type matter more than brand. A one-third horsepower unit handles light use. One-half to three-quarters horsepower suits a family that cooks daily. Continuous-feed units run while you add scraps, and batch-feed units run only with the stopper in place, which some people prefer for safety.

Insulated units run quieter, and stainless steel grinding parts resist corrosion. Check that the new unit's mount matches your sink flange, or plan for a new mounting kit.

What the Plumber Checks Beyond the Unit

A disposal sits on top of several other parts. The plumber checks the dishwasher drain connection, the trap and the line to the wall, since a partial blockage downstream makes a healthy disposal look broken. If the sink drains slowly even with a new unit, the branch line needs cabling, which is covered under drain cleaning.

Electrical and Plumbing Standards for Disposals

A disposal touches two systems. The drain connection follows plumbing rules, and the power supply follows electrical rules. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. Any new outlet or wiring change near a sink should be checked by a qualified electrician, and the dishwasher drain connection must be installed so wastewater cannot flow back.
