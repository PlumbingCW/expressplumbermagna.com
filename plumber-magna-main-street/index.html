Magna Main Street is the oldest part of town, and the plumbing questions here come from older homes, historic storefronts and the small restaurants between them. This page covers the original townsite along Main Street, which is West 2700 South, and the residential blocks around it.

If water is spraying or sewage is backing up, call now. The shutoff steps further down apply to any building, old or new.

What Main Street Means in Magna

Magna began as a farming community called Pleasant Green in 1868 and took the name Magna in 1915. The town center grew around Main Street, and early neighborhoods there carried names such as Greektown, Japantown and Little Italy. Those names come from the immigrant families who settled near the mills.

You will not find an official boundary for the area. We describe it by its anchors instead: Main Street itself, the Empress Theatre at 9104 West 2700 South and the older blocks that surround them.

Today Main Street is a mix of storefronts, restaurants, churches and fraternal halls with homes on the nearby streets. A local real estate guide describes the original downtown as bungalows and ranch-style homes from the early 1900s, some of which may need renovation. That is a description of the market, not a survey of any one house.

Older Buildings and What They Mean for Plumbing

Age alone does not tell you what is inside a wall. A house from the early 1900s may still have original pipe, may have been repiped in sections over the decades or may have been fully updated. The only way to know is to look, which is why we start with the fixture and the shutoff rather than a guess.

Where old and new pipe meet, the joints deserve attention. Mixed materials, unlabeled valves and shutoffs that have not been turned in years are common reasons a small repair takes longer than expected.

After the 2020 Earthquake

The March 18, 2020 magnitude 5.7 earthquake damaged several historic buildings along Main Street, according to the city. Unreinforced brick buildings were among the hardest hit across Magna. After any strong shaking, plumbing can be affected even when walls look fine.

Check for a wet spot, a drop in water pressure or the sound of running water with everything off. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Check these after a quake or any building repair

  • Water meter: with every fixture off, a moving dial means a leak.
  • Ceilings and walls below bathrooms for new stains or soft spots.
  • Water heater connections and the pipe to the drain.
  • Floor drains and the lowest fixture for slow draining.
  • Any area near new cracks in foundations or masonry.

Restaurants, Shops and Mixed Buildings

Many Main Street properties hold a business below and living space above, or a business in a former house. That mix puts kitchens, restrooms and floor drains close to older supply lines. Grease is the usual cause of kitchen drain trouble, and it builds up faster than most owners expect.

Whether you own the building or lease it, tell dispatch which fixture is affected and whether the space is open to customers. We handle commercial plumbing calls the same way as home calls: find the cause, state the price, then fix it.

Digging, Utilities and Who to Call First

Before any digging for a water or sewer repair, call Blue Stakes of Utah. Utah requires three business days of notice, and the marks stay valid for 21 calendar days. A line repair near Main Street can cross several older utilities, so the marks matter.

For service line trouble, see our water line repair and sewer line repair pages. Magna Water District provides water and sewer service in Magna, and your bill shows which provider covers your address.
