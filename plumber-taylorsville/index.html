Start With Your Water District

Taylorsville-Bennion Improvement District supplies water to the city. The district reported in its 2024 water quality report that most of its supply is pumped from wells in the Salt Lake Valley aquifer, with the rest purchased from Jordan Valley Water Conservancy District. It serves more than 17,200 connections, and it considers all of its service lines to be non-lead.

That matters when a plumber opens a wall. The district's tap samples in 2023 showed copper and lead from household plumbing within action levels, so the pipes in your house are the part to watch.

Taylorsville's Neighborhood Mix

Taylorsville incorporated in 1996 from the Taylorsville-Bennion area and part of Kearns, after rapid residential and apartment growth in the 1980s and early 1990s. That growth means a mix of ages. Older Bennion and Taylorsville homes may still have original galvanized or copper lines, while condos and apartments from the growth years have shared stacks and water heaters that fail on a schedule.

Condo and apartment owners should know that shutoffs for a unit are sometimes controlled by the association. Ask your property manager where the building's main shutoff is.

Cold Weather and Taylorsville Pipes

Salt Lake Valley winters freeze exterior walls and crawlspaces. Open the cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls, keep the heat on, and unhook garden hoses. If a pipe freezes and you are not sure whether it split, shut off the main and call before you thaw it.

What To Tell Dispatch

Give your ZIP, your cross streets and the type of property. In a condo or apartment, mention the unit and whether you can reach the building shutoff. Describe the water: clear, brown or sewage. Tell the dispatcher about anyone who is elderly, medically fragile or without other housing, so the call is handled with the right urgency.

Old Pipes, New Pipes, Shared Pipes

Taylorsville's single-family homes tend to have supply lines inside walls that the owner controls. Condos and townhomes add shared stacks, shared water heaters and shared shutoffs governed by an association. A leak that starts upstairs can damage three units, so quick shutoff and quick notice matter more than who owes what.

Preparing for Cold Snaps

Before a hard freeze, open the cabinet doors on exterior walls, let a faucet drip on the coldest wall, and disconnect hoses. Know where your main shutoff is and test that it turns. If you will be away, ask a neighbor to check the house or set the thermostat no lower than the temperature your plumber recommends. A frozen line is easier to prevent than to repair.

Codes and Permits in Taylorsville

Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. The city's building office handles permits and inspections in Taylorsville, so major plumbing work such as a water heater replacement or a bathroom addition should be confirmed with it. The plumber can tell you what the work needs before it starts.
