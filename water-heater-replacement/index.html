A failed water heater is an emergency the moment it floods a garage or leaves a family without heat in winter. If your tank is leaking, turn off the cold supply valve above it and the power or gas to the unit, then call the 24/7 line. A replacement can often be scheduled the same day when a slot is open, including evenings and weekends.

Keep towels at the base and move stored items out of the water. Do not run the dishwasher or washer until the tank is drained.

Signs the Tank Is Done

Water at the base

A seam or tank wall has corroded through. No patch fixes this.

Rust-colored hot water

The anode rod is gone and the inner steel is rusting.

Age past about ten years

Efficiency drops and failure risk climbs each year.

Repeated repairs

A second repair in a short time points to a tired tank.

Choosing the Right Replacement

Size comes first. A household of two to three people often does well with a 40 gallon tank, while a family of five usually needs 50 gallons or more. The first-hour rating on the yellow label matters more than the gallon number, because it tells you how much hot water you really get in a busy morning.

Moving from gas to a heat pump or tankless model changes venting, gas sizing and electrical needs, and sometimes the permit. Ask your city building department what a conversion requires before you commit. Our tankless water heater page covers that choice.

Hard Water Shortens Heater Life in Magna

The 2023 Magna Water District report shows moderately hard water, with hardness averaging 105 mg/L and swings from 58 to 185 mg/L between samples. Scale builds on the tank bottom and the elements.

Pairing a new heater with an annual flush, and thinking about a water softener if scale is already heavy at your faucets, protects the investment. A new anode rod costs little and slows tank corrosion.

What a Replacement Visit Includes

From shutoff to hot water

  • Shut off water and gas or power, then drain the old tank.
  • Disconnect and haul away the old unit.
  • Set the new heater, with a pan where the location calls for one.
  • Connect supply lines, relief valve and discharge pipe to code.
  • Test venting or wiring, fill the tank, and check for leaks at every joint.

An expansion tank is often required when the home has a pressure-reducing valve or a check valve on the supply, because heated water has nowhere to expand. The plumber checks that at the start and tells you if one is needed.
