A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so it never runs out the way a tank can. It also asks more of your gas line, your vent and your water. We install tankless units, repair the common faults and flush the heat exchanger so scale does not shorten its life.

How a Tankless Heater Works, and Why It Matters

When a hot tap opens, a flow sensor fires the burner and water passes through a heat exchanger, a coil of tubing that moves heat to the water. There is no storage tank, so there is no standby heat loss and no tank to rust through.

The trade-off is that the exchanger is a narrow passage. Minerals that would settle harmlessly in a tank bake onto the tubing instead, and that scale is the single most common cause of tankless failure.

Gas Lines, Venting and Sizing

Tankless burners pull far more gas than a tank. An older half-inch gas line that served a tank heater often cannot carry enough for a tankless unit, and upsizing means opening walls or running new pipe. Vent material is also specific to the unit, so a plumber should confirm the manufacturer's requirement rather than reusing an old flue.

Questions to settle before a tankless install

  • How many fixtures will run at once at your busiest hour?
  • Can the existing gas meter and line supply the required input?
  • What vent type does the unit need, and where can it terminate?
  • Is there a condensate drain nearby for a condensing model?
  • Do you want a recirculation loop for faster hot water at far fixtures?

Flushing and Descaling in Magna Water

Magna Water District reported hardness averaging 105 mg/L in 2023, with total dissolved solids averaging 639 mg/L. That is moderately hard water with a lot of dissolved minerals, which is exactly the condition that scales a heat exchanger.

A yearly descaling flush circulates a mild acid through the unit for about an hour and removes the film. Many manufacturers require it to keep the warranty valid, so keep a record. A water softener reduces how fast scale returns.

Install Standards Worth Knowing

Tankless units add gas and venting loads that tank heaters do not. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. Gas line size, vent materials and condensate disposal are the details that decide whether an install passes inspection, so ask your plumber to walk through them.

What to do if the unit stops heating

  • Write down the error code on the display.
  • Check the power, the gas valve and the isolation valves.
  • Open one hot tap fully to test the flow.
  • Shut the unit off if you smell gas and leave the house.
  • Call a plumber if a reset does not clear the code.
