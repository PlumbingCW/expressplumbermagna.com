A failed sump pump during a storm or snowmelt can put inches of water in a basement within hours. If water is rising, do not walk through it if the electrical panel or outlets are low, and call the 24/7 line. We handle sump pump emergencies at night and on weekends.

Sump Pump Emergency: What to Do

While you wait

  • Check whether the pump is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped.
  • Check that the float moves freely in the pit.
  • Look at the discharge line outside for ice or blockage.
  • Move belongings off the floor.
  • Do not touch wet electrical equipment. Shut off power at the panel only if you can reach it dry.

Magna Groundwater and Snowmelt

The Salt Lake Valley's shallow groundwater varies by area, and homes on the lower benches near the lake plain see higher water tables than those near the foothills. Spring snowmelt from the Oquirrh Mountains raises the water table, which is when many pumps are tested hardest.

Check your pit before March. A pump that sat dry all winter may be stuck.

Battery Backup and Water-Powered Options

Pumps stop when the power goes out, and storms often cause outages. A battery backup pump or a water-powered backup keeps the pit from overflowing during an outage. Battery systems need testing and replacement every few years, while water-powered units run off house pressure but use water.

If water enters despite a working pump, the cause may be exterior drainage, covered under basement waterproofing. A broken discharge line may also be a water line matter, depending on the route.

Discharge Lines and Where the Water Goes

Discharge must carry water away from the foundation, not into the sewer, which most cities ban. Check that the line slopes away, ends well from the house, and has a check valve so water does not drain back. In winter, a buried or insulated line avoids freeze-ups.

Testing a Sump Pump Before It Matters

Check the outlet

Make sure the pump is plugged into a working, protected outlet.

Pour in water

Add a bucket of water to the pit until the float rises.

Watch the cycle

The pump should start, empty the pit and shut off cleanly.

Check the discharge

Walk outside and confirm water is leaving the pipe and moving away.

Test the backup

Unplug the main pump briefly and confirm the backup starts.

If the pump hums but does not move water, the impeller may be jammed or the check valve stuck. If it cycles every few seconds, the check valve may be missing and water is draining back into the pit.

Pit Care and Float Position

The pit should be clear of silt, gravel and debris, and the float should rise and fall without touching the walls. A pump sitting on gravel draws grit into the impeller, so it should rest on a flat paver or a solid base. A cover on the pit keeps out debris and reduces humidity in the basement. These small details extend the life of a pump more than the brand name.

Where Sump Water May Go

Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. That code and local sewer rules together limit where pump discharge can go, and many places prohibit sending groundwater to the sanitary sewer. Ask your sewer provider and building department before you connect the discharge to anything other than a yard outlet.
