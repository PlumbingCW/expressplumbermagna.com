Installing a water heater is a planned job done best before the old one fails. Whether you are finishing a basement, building an addition or upgrading an old tank, the install sets how long the heater lasts and how safe it is. We handle gas and electric tank installs, including venting, gas connections and the parts that code calls for.

Gas Tank Installations in Magna Homes

Most gas installs hinge on two things, the vent and the gas line. The vent must rise continuously to the outside and clear combustible materials, and the gas line has to be large enough for the new burner. Old flues from a previous heater are often rusted or undersized for a modern unit.

The plumber checks the vent path before quoting, because a vent rebuild changes the price more than the heater itself. Carbon monoxide from a poor vent is not a risk worth cutting corners on.

Electric Tank Installations

An electric install is simpler but still depends on the circuit. A 240 volt dedicated circuit, a correct breaker and a disconnect within reach are the usual requirements, and an electrician may need to do the wiring. Ask your plumber to say who is responsible for each step so nothing falls between trades.

What Gets Installed Alongside the Tank

Parts a code-compliant install usually includes

  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size discharge pipe to a safe location.
  • Seismic strapping where the area requires it, which matters in an earthquake region like the Wasatch Front.
  • Expansion tank when the home has a closed plumbing system.
  • Shutoff valves and flexible connectors on both supplies.
  • A drain pan and drain line where a leak would reach finished floors.

The March 2020 magnitude 5.7 earthquake near Magna is a reminder that a tall tank can shift on its own supply lines. Ask your plumber about strapping and flexible connectors whenever a heater is moved or replaced.

Sizing and Fuel Choice

A household of two or three generally fits a 40 gallon tank and a family of four or five a 50 gallon unit, but morning peaks, soaker tubs and large-flow fixtures change that. If you are comparing tank and tankless, read the tankless water heater guide before you decide.

Standards for Water Heater Installation

Water heaters are covered by plumbing, gas and electrical rules at once. Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. Combustion air, venting, relief valve discharge and gas connections are the details inspectors care about, and a permit is commonly required for installing or replacing a tank. Ask the plumber who pulls it.
