Most faucet problems are small, but a failed supply line or a shutoff valve that will not close can flood a kitchen in minutes. If water is spraying under a sink and the little valves will not turn, shut off the main water valve for the house, then call the 24/7 line. We take fixture emergencies at night and on weekends.

When a Fixture Problem Is an Emergency

Treat it as urgent when a supply line is spraying, when a valve will not close and water keeps flowing, or when a shower valve is stuck on hot. A steady drip from a faucet spout is a daytime appointment, not an emergency. We tell you which one you have on the phone.

Moderately hard water leaves its mark on fixtures. Magna Water District reported hardness averaging 105 mg/L in 2023, and total dissolved solids near 639 mg/L. Crusty white rings at the aerator and stiff handles are the usual signs, and a cartridge swap often restores a faucet that seemed ready for the trash.

Shutoff Valves and Supply Lines

The small stop valves under sinks and behind toilets seize if they are never turned. When they do not close, a plumber replaces the valve, often with a quarter-turn ball valve that works for years. Old braided supply lines and rubber hoses also fail, sometimes catastrophically, and are cheap to replace before they do.

A corroded valve is not urgent until it fails, and then it is. Replacing it at the first sign of corrosion is the cheaper path.

Sinks, Strainers and Tub Spouts

A leak under the sink might be the basket strainer, the P-trap or the drain tailpiece rather than the faucet. A tub or shower that drains slowly or drips from the diverter has its own fixes. If the sink drain itself is clogged, see drain cleaning.

Before you call, check these

  • Is the leak at the faucet, the supply line or the drain below?
  • Do the shutoff valves turn, or are they stuck?
  • How old is the fixture, and is it still sold?
  • Do you want to repair the fixture or replace it?
  • Can the plumber reach the area, with the cabinet emptied?

Choosing Fixtures That Are Easy to Live With

If you are replacing a faucet, pick one with a standard cartridge that is sold in hardware stores and a removable aerator. Fixtures with proprietary parts can become scrap when a single piece fails. In moderately hard water, a model whose spout end unscrews for cleaning will keep its flow for years.

Ask the plumber to bring a few options that fit your existing holes. Matching the hole count and spread avoids drilling a new sink or countertop.

A Five Minute Look That Prevents a Flood

Try these once a year

  • Turn each shutoff valve a quarter turn and back to keep it from seizing.
  • Look at braided supply lines for bulges, rust or frayed wire.
  • Press a dry paper towel against connections to check for seepage.
  • Run the faucet and look under the sink while it runs.
  • Replace any line or valve that is more than a decade old.
