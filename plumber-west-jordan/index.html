A Year of Plumbing in West Jordan

Winter freezes lines in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls. Spring snowmelt from the Oquirrh Mountains raises groundwater and tests sump pumps. Summer brings sprinkler line breaks and water heater strain from guests. Fall is the time to disconnect hoses and drain outdoor bibs before the first hard freeze.

Calls follow that calendar, so preventive work pays off in specific months.

A seasonal plumbing checklist

  • Fall: disconnect hoses and shut off outdoor bibs.
  • Winter: open cabinet doors on outside walls during hard freezes.
  • Early spring: test your sump pump before snowmelt.
  • Summer: check sprinkler lines for leaks and look at the water heater.
  • Anytime: know where your main shutoff is.

West Jordan's Own Water Department

West Jordan City runs its own water department, which takes more than 100 water samples each month and publishes an annual water quality report. Because it is a city utility, your bill and any main break notice come from the city. If water is surfacing near the street, call the city. If it is on your side of the meter, call us.

Hardness numbers are in the city's report. We do not quote a single figure, since supply varies.

Old and New Housing

West Jordan holds older neighborhoods near the original town center and newer subdivisions out toward the west bench and the Mountain View Corridor. Older areas may have galvanized supply pipe and clay laterals. Newer homes have PEX and PVC but face other issues, such as water heaters in tight closets and sump pit problems on higher water tables.

Nearby, the former company town of Bacchus, built in 1915 around a powder plant on the valley's western edge, is a reminder of how much this edge of the valley changed. It is a ghost town today, but the pipes of the neighborhoods that replaced such places are decades old.

Sump Pits on the West Bench

Homes on the west side of West Jordan, closer to the foothills, can see seasonal groundwater that older neighborhoods farther east do not. If your home has a sump pit, test it before March and again after the first big storm. A pit that sat dry all winter may have a stuck float or a clogged intake.

What Goes Wrong in Newer Homes

Newer West Jordan subdivisions have PEX supply lines, PVC drains and modern fixtures, so the typical calls differ from older areas. Water heaters in tight closets, failed shutoff valves, a loose toilet flange and kitchen drains blocked by construction debris are common. These are fast fixes when caught early.

Calling From a Business

Shops and offices near Jordan Landing and along Bangerter Highway can call the same line. Tell dispatch whether the building has a landlord or manager, whether a kitchen or restroom is affected and how to get inside after hours. Commercial calls are routed to a plumber who can bring the right equipment the first time.

Codes and Permits in West Jordan

Utah applies the International Plumbing Code (IPC) with statewide amendments, and your city or county building department enforces it. West Jordan City manages permits for work inside its limits, so projects such as sump pump discharge changes or new bathrooms should be confirmed with the city. We explain what a job involves before it begins.
