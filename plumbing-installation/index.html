Plumbing installation covers anything new: a bathroom addition, a basement bar sink, a laundry hookup, a hose bib, a pressure regulator or a sewer cleanout. Planning it right avoids trouble at inspection. Call before you buy fixtures, since rough-in dimensions and venting limit what fits.

Basements and Below-Sewer Fixtures

Many homes in Magna have basements, and a bathroom below the sewer line cannot drain by gravity. An ejector pump in a sealed pit lifts waste to the main drain, and the pit needs venting. The design is an easy thing to get wrong, which is why it belongs with a plumber.

Permits, Inspections and Code

New plumbing work is permitted in most Utah cities and counties, and inspectors check rough-in before walls are closed. Utah's plumbing licensing is handled by DOPL, the Division of Professional Licensing, so ask any plumber about licensing before hiring. We pull permits where needed and schedule inspections.

Doing it right the first time is cheaper than opening finished walls to fix a failed inspection.

Water Quality and Fixture Choice

Magna Water District's 2023 report listed hardness averaging 105 mg/L. That is moderately hard, so choose fixtures with removable aerators and cartridges you can service. A water softener helps in a larger remodel.

A new water heater is a separate project, covered on water heater installation. For repairs to existing fixtures, see faucet and sink repair.

Rough-In, Then Finish: How a Project Is Sequenced

Plumbing happens in two stages. The rough-in comes first, when walls and floors are open. The plumber sets drains, vents and supply lines, then pressure-tests the system so an inspector can see it. After the walls are closed and finished, the plumber returns to set fixtures, connect supply lines and test everything again.

Mistakes That Cost the Most to Fix

The expensive errors are all decided before fixtures arrive. A toilet ordered with the wrong rough-in distance will not fit the flange. A vanity set against a drain that lands inside the cabinet back needs the drain moved. A shower valve set too deep for the finished tile stands off the wall.

Share your fixture model numbers before the walls close and ask the plumber to check them against the rough-in. It takes a few minutes and saves a return trip.

Working With Other Trades

Plumbing shares space with framing, electrical and ventilation. A bathroom fan duct, a joist notch and a drain line all want the same cavity. A good plumber walks the job with the contractor so framing is cut for the drain, not the reverse. If you are acting as your own general contractor, schedule the plumber before drywall, not after.
